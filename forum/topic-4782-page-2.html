Reporter: In Konjiki no Gashu's case, can you tell us what kind of steps it goes through to be complete?
Raiku: We start from discussing. If I tell my editor the story I'm thinking about, the editor comments whether the story is "usable or not", and sometimes the editor suggest a story. Something like "How about going like this? Why don't you go like this?"
Reporter: So, it's like in form of suggestion?
Raiku: Yes. I accept it if it's a really good idea. That way, I don't have to use up my ideas so that my life as mangaka gets longer. But if I am forced to do like the fourth editor I mentioned in my affidavit, it sometimes goes to worse direction. So after we discuss like that and decide where to end roughly in 18 pages, I go to family restaurant around 7 to 8 at night and start working there until next morning, eating two meals. If it goes well, I finish it in two days, and I send it to the editorial department after finishing, and show it to the editor and if he says "OK", then I start working on details. If he tells me to fix it, I send them by fax until he says "OK". So when the conti(rough draft) is finished, I call assistants and start drawing on the paper by looking at that conti.
Reporter: Right. Other mangakas' working stories sometimes contain "I stay in family restaurant to...". Why family restaurant?
Raiku: Probably because there are 'free beverages' (laughs).
Reporter: (Laughs)
Raiku: If I draw at home, there are a lot of allurements. There's TV, mangas and computers, so say, my mind gets loosened up. There are other things than work so I keep on lagging by resting. In family restaurant, 'only work is there' so I can concentrate. And even though I don't know any of them but people watch me so, if I was in my own area, I would do whatever I want, but I tend to do well if there are people around me.
Reporter: So there are enough tensions around?
Raiku: Yes. I think that's why other mangakas prefer to go to family restaurants as well.
Reporter: Right. It sounds very convincing. And you've mentioned before that you use assistants, how many do you use up to?
Raiku: Maximum 5. Usually, it's 4, but when we were really busy, we called one subsitute to be five. For most cases, four is enough.
Reporter: What do you consider when selecting an assistant?
Raiku: Personality comes first. Saying greetings often is nice. And for ability, they have to be able to do minimum work so I tell them to do coloring, thick lines, use White or tone to see their speed. If the simple works are slow, we can't get things done in time in weekly magazines. If the quality is above the certain point, I hire. And I can't use a newcomer's picture if they don't fix it. They get better by fixing here and there anf background. If they don't have speed here, it's hard to raise them. So a certain amount of good nature and speed are important.
Reporter: If it's a weekly magazine, then is there a day you can rest in a week?
Raiku: If conti goes well, then I can rest. (laughs)
Reporter: So if conti gets finished quickly, you can rest and if not, things get hard.
Raiku: Yes. Conti is everything. The amount of time I spend for drawing doesn't change. It's always 18 pages and four assistants help me. If the picture gets complicated above certain point, we put out background a bit, we even our paces like that so... still there are hard times and easy times. When there are a lot of characters, it never seem to end and if it's just everyday life scene, it's just Gash and Kiyomaro so it gets very easy.
